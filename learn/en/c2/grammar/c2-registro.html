C2 · Grammar

Register and style: formal vs colloquial

How the same meaning sounds in a formal letter and in a chat with a friend

Formal y coloquial

Formal

Tú, usted, vosotros, ustedes

Usted / ustedes is the polite form: third-person verbs and the pronouns le, les, lo, la, su. In Spain tú is very widespread; usted is for official situations, older people and customers. In much of Latin America usted is more frequent and vosotros is not used at all: ustedes is both the polite and the friendly plural.

In Argentina, Uruguay and Central America people say vos instead of tú, with verb forms of its own: vos tenés, vos sabés. The polite usted is the same as everywhere else.

Keep the register

Keep the register consistent: don’t start with usted and switch to te or tu. Señores, les ruego que tomen asiento — everything is in the ustedes form.

The grammar of formal style

Polite conditional

Les agradecería que…

The conditional softens a request and is followed by the imperfect subjunctive: Les agradecería que me enviaran…

Nouns instead of verbs

a la llegada del ministro

A verbal noun instead of a clause: a la llegada del ministro instead of cuando llegó el ministro.

Passive and impersonal

se ruega · ha sido aprobada

The doer stays in the background: Se ruega silencio, Su solicitud ha sido aprobada.

Dropping que

Le ruego disculpe…

After rogar, agradecer, suplicar letters sometimes drop que: Le ruego disculpe las molestias.

Colloquial

What makes speech colloquial

Clipped words: el finde, la tele, el profe, la bici, el cole. Intensifiers: súper, un montón de, mogollón (Spain). Fillers: pues, o sea, bueno, vale, ¿sabes?. Diminutives soften things: un momentito, un cafecito.

The polite imperfecto makes a soft request in a shop or on the phone: Quería una barra de pan, Quería preguntarle una cosa. It is neutral and polite, fine in any spoken exchange.

The grammar of speech

Polite imperfecto

Quería una barra de pan.

The past tense softens the wish: it sounds more modest than quiero.

Que at the start

Que ya voy. · ¡Que te calles!

Que repeats or insists on what has been said.

Es que…

Es que no tengo tiempo.

An excuse or explanation: “the thing is…”.

Emphatic pronoun

Se bebió toda la botella.

Se, me stress that the action is complete or that the speaker is involved: se lo comió todo.

Where it doesn’t fit

Currar, mogollón, flipar are fine with friends, but not in a letter to the bank or at a job interview. The polite quería and diminutives, on the other hand, suit almost any spoken exchange.

Vocabulary in three registers

Formal vocabulary uses bookish synonyms: solicitar for pedir, remitir for mandar, dar comienzo for empezar. Between the colloquial and the bookish word there is almost always a neutral one — choose it when in doubt.

The register check

Ask yourself: would I say this to a judge or a bank manager? If not, look for the neutral or the bookish word.

And the other way round: dar comienzo or remitir in a chat with a friend sound like a joke or irony.

Letters

How a letter is built

A letter opens with Estimado/a señor/a: or Estimados señores: (with a colon) and closes with Atentamente, / Un cordial saludo,. Typical formulas: Por la presente…, Quedo a la espera de su respuesta, a la mayor brevedad.

Formal letter formulas

Set phrases

Por la presente · En respuesta a su escrito · a la mayor brevedad · Agradeciendo de antemano su atención · Sin otro particular · Reciba un cordial saludo · Le saluda atentamente

Punctuation and abbreviations

After the greeting comes a colon, and the text starts on a new line with a capital letter. A comma after the greeting is a calque from English.

Abbreviations: Sr., Sra., Srta., D., D.ª, Ud. (Vd.). Ud. takes a capital and a full stop, while the full word usted is written in lower case.

The skeleton of a formal letter

1) Estimado/a…: 2) Me dirijo a usted para… 3) the point, with the request in the conditional 4) Quedo a la espera de su respuesta. 5) Atentamente, + your name.

Examples

Formal

  • Les agradecería que me enviaran la factura a la mayor brevedad.I would be grateful if you could send me the invoice as soon as possible.
  • Por la presente le comunicamos que su solicitud ha sido aprobada.We hereby inform you that your application has been approved.
  • Le rogamos disculpe las molestias.We apologise for any inconvenience.
  • Le agradeceríamos que confirmara su asistencia.We would be grateful if you could confirm your attendance.
  • Tras la aprobación del presupuesto, se iniciarán las obras.Once the budget has been approved, work will begin.
  • Se ruega a los señores pasajeros que permanezcan sentados.Passengers are kindly requested to remain seated.
  • La directora desempeñó su cargo con gran profesionalidad.The director performed her duties with great professionalism.
  • Les informamos de que el servicio quedará interrumpido el lunes.Please be advised that the service will be suspended on Monday.

Colloquial

  • Este finde me voy a la playa con un montón de amigos.This weekend I’m off to the beach with loads of friends.
  • Espera un momentito, que ya voy.Hang on a sec, I’m coming.
  • Buenos días, quería pedir cita con el médico.Good morning, I’d like to make a doctor’s appointment.
  • Es que no he pegado ojo en toda la noche.It’s just that I didn’t sleep a wink all night.
  • ¡Que te calles, que no me dejas oír!Will you be quiet? I can’t hear a thing!
  • El niño se comió toda la tarta él solito.The kid polished off the whole cake all by himself.
  • O sea, que al final no venís, ¿no?So in the end you’re not coming, right?
  • Mañana no puedo, tengo que currar hasta las diez.I can’t tomorrow, I’ve got to work till ten.

The same idea in two registers

  • Solicito información sobre el curso de verano.I am writing to request information about the summer course.
  • ¿Me pasas la info del curso de verano?Can you send me the info on the summer course?
  • La reunión dará comienzo a las nueve en punto.The meeting will commence at nine o’clock sharp.
  • La reunión arranca a las nueve.The meeting kicks off at nine.
  • Nos vemos obligados a rescindir su contrato.We are obliged to terminate your contract.
  • Me han echado del curro.I got the sack.
  • La noticia causó gran asombro entre los asistentes.The news caused great astonishment among those present.
  • La gente flipó con la noticia.People were blown away by the news.
