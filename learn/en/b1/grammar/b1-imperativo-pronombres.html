Imperativo and pronouns

Affirmative and negative commands, where pronouns go, and the accent rules — from zero to automatic

Imperativo y Pronombres

Affirmative

Affirmative commands are requests, orders and advice in the positive form: “do it”, “buy it”, “sit down”.

Whose forms

tú

habla · come · vive

Take the él / ella form of the present indicative (no -s): él habla → ¡habla!

usted · ustedes · nosotros

hable · hablen · hablemos

The matching present subjunctive form — simply without “que”.

vosotros

hablar → hablad

The infinitive with the final -r changed to -d: comer → comed, vivir → vivid.

The usted, ustedes, nosotros and vosotros forms of these verbs are regular — from the subjunctive or with -d: diga, haced, salgamos.

Negative

How it is formed

For every person

no + subjuntivo

No exceptions, even for tú. Negative commands have no forms of their own — just the negation + present subjunctive.

tú ≠ tú

habla → no hables

The tú form in affirmative and negative commands is almost always different: come → no comas, ven → no vengas.

Negative commands

hablarcomervivir
túno hablesno comasno vivas

vosotrosno habléisno comáisno viváis

In practice: since this is the subjunctive, all irregular subjunctive stems carry over to negative commands automatically: ser → no seas, ir → no vayas, tener → no tengas, saber → no sepas.

The stem changes in tú, usted and ustedes — just as in the indicative and subjunctive. In nosotros and vosotros cerrar and volver keep their stem; -ir verbs change e→i, o→u in nosotros: pidamos, durmamos.

Position

This is where most mistakes happen: the pronoun either sticks to the verb or stands on its own, depending on the type of command.

Two positions

After the verb

Cómprala. Dímelo. Sentaos.

Verb + pronoun = one word.

Before the verb

No la compres. No me lo digas.

no + pronoun + verb, all written separately. No os sentéis.

Two pronouns: the RID rule

OrderWhat it isForms
1 · Rreflexiveme, te, se, nos, os
2 · Iindirect (to whom?)me, te, se, nos, os
3 · Ddirect (what?)lo, la, los, las

The RID rule (Reflexive → Indirect → Direct): with two pronouns, the reflexive or indirect one (me, te, se, nos, os) comes first, then the direct one (lo, la, los, las). Negative commands keep the same order, but before the verb.

The golden rule le / les → se: before lo, la, los, las, le and les always turn into se. Pídeselo (not “pídelelo”), dáselas (not “dálelas”), never “lelo”.

Stress

When a pronoun is attached to an affirmative command, the word gets longer — and the stress often has to be “rescued” with a written accent.

The logic: a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the second-to-last syllable by default. When we attach a pronoun, the verb’s stress must stay where it was — if it is no longer second-to-last, we add a written accent.

Three groups

1 pronoun + 1 syllable

dame · hazlo · ponte · dile

A one-syllable tú form + one pronoun — usually no accent.

1 pronoun + 2 or more syllables

cómpralo · escríbeme · dígalo

A form of two or more syllables (compra, escribe, diga…) + a pronoun — with an accent.

2 pronouns

dámelo · díselo · póntelo

An accent almost always, even with a one-syllable form.

Negative commands are easier: the pronouns stand separately before the verb, and the subjunctive form keeps its usual stress — no me lo digas, no te lo pongas, no se los des.

Reflexive

Reflexive verbs (levantarse, sentarse, irse…) lose one letter before -nos and -os in affirmative commands: nosotros drops the -s, vosotros drops the -d.

Exception 1 — irse: nosotros uses the colloquial vámonos (let’s go), not “vayámonos”. It is the only case like this.

Exception 2 — irse: vosotros uses the special form idos instead of the expected “íos” — the only case where the -d stays.

The stress stays put: after losing the -s, the nosotros form almost always needs an accent — levantémonos, sentémonos, vámonos: the word got longer, but the stress stayed on the same syllable. In negative commands the pronoun goes before the verb as usual.

Reflexive verbs in use

  • Levántate. → No te levantes.Get up. → Don’t get up.
  • Quedaos con nosotros. → No os quedéis con ellos.Stay with us. → Don’t stay with them. (vosotros)
  • ¡Vámonos, que es tarde!Let’s go, it’s late!
  • Pónganse cómodos, por favor.Make yourselves comfortable, please. (ustedes)

Examples

Worked examples: yes → no

  • Cómprate una lavadora. → No te la compres.Buy yourself a washing machine. → Don’t buy it.
  • Pídele las llaves a tu padre. → No se las pidas.Ask your father for the keys. → Don’t ask him for them. (le → se, las = the keys)
  • Haz mucho ruido. → No hagas mucho.Make a lot of noise. → Don’t make much. (ruido is not replaced by a pronoun)
  • Tira la basura. → No la tires.Throw out the rubbish. → Don’t throw it out.
  • Ponle más sal a la salsa. → No le pongas más.Put more salt in the sauce. → Don’t put in any more. (le stays le: no lo / la / los / las follows)
  • Quédate con tus amigos. → No te quedes con ellos.Stay with your friends. → Don’t stay with them. (don’t drop the reflexive te)
  • Ponte este queso en la pasta. → No te lo pongas.Put this cheese on your pasta. → Don’t put it on.
  • Quítalos de la mesa. → No los quites de ahí.Take them off the table (your feet). → Don’t move them from there.

Affirmative and negative

  • Ven aquí ahora mismo.Come here right now.
  • Haced los deberes antes de cenar.Do your homework before dinner. (vosotros)
  • Vuelva usted mañana, por favor.Please come back tomorrow.
  • Córtate la melena. → No te la cortes.Cut your long hair. → Don’t cut it. (cortes is subjunctive)
  • No tengas miedo, no pasa nada.Don’t be afraid, it’s all right.
  • No abramos la ventana, hace frío.Let’s not open the window, it’s cold.
  • No salgan sin paraguas.Don’t go out without an umbrella. (ustedes)

Pronoun position

  • Cómprala. → No la compres.Buy it. → Don’t buy it.
  • Dímelo. → No me lo digas.Tell me. → Don’t tell me.
  • Sentaos. → No os sentéis.Sit down. → Don’t sit down. (vosotros)
  • Dame el libro. → Dámelo.Give me the book. → Give it to me. (indirect + direct)
  • Dile la verdad a él. → Díselo.Tell him the truth. → Tell it to him. (le + lo → se lo)
  • Ponte el jersey. → Póntelo.Put on your jumper. → Put it on. (reflexive te + direct lo)
  • No se lo digas.Don’t tell him. (same order, but before the verb)

Checklist before you answer:
1) Affirmative or negative? — choose the verb form.
2) Is the verb reflexive (ponerse, quedarse…)? — keep its pronoun.
3) Is there a direct object (what?) — replace it with lo, la, los, las.
4) Is there an indirect object (to whom?) — me, te, le→se, nos, os, les→se, placed before the direct one.
5) Affirmative form — attach the pronouns and check the accent.

Three formulas

Di Haz Ve Pon Sal Sé Ten Ven — the eight irregular tú forms.

No — always the subjunctive, with the pronouns before the verb.
