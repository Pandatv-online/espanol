A2 · Grammar

Reflexive verbs

Verbs with -se: levantarse, ducharse, llamarse — and where to put me, te, se

Verbos reflexivos

How it works

A reflexive verb has -se at the end of the infinitive: levantarse (to get up), ducharse (to shower), llamarse (to be called). The action is done to oneself.

When conjugated, se changes with the person — me, te, se, nos, os, se — and goes before the verb.

How it works

Pronoun + verb

me + levanto

The pronoun matches the person of the verb: yo me levanto, tú te levantas. Never “levanto se”.

Se is 3rd person

él se · ellos se · usted se

Se is only for él, ella, usted, ellos, ellas, ustedes. “I” takes me, “we” takes nos: nos levantamos, not “se levantamos”.

Presente in use

  • Me levanto a las siete.I get up at seven.
  • ¿Cómo te llamas?What’s your name?
  • Los viernes nos acostamos tarde.On Fridays we go to bed late.

Movement and change

“Each other”: in the plural, nos, os, se can show a mutual action: Nos vemos mañana. — “See you tomorrow”. Ana y Luis se quieren. — “Ana and Luis love each other”.

Conjugation

Switch the tense for each verb. In the Perfecto the pronoun goes before haber: me he levantado, and the participle does not change. In the Indefinido it goes before the verb as usual: me levanté.

Stem changes work just as with ordinary verbs: acostarse, dormirse (o → ue), despertarse, sentarse (e → ie), vestirse (e → i). The nosotros and vosotros forms keep the original stem: nos acostamos.

In different tenses

  • Hoy me he levantado tarde.I got up late today.
  • ¿Ya te has duchado?Have you had a shower yet?
  • Los niños se han vestido solos.The children have got dressed by themselves.
  • Anoche nos acostamos a las dos.Last night we went to bed at two.
  • Mi abuelo se despierta muy temprano.My grandfather wakes up very early.

Pronoun

With a normal verb form, put it before the verb: Me ducho. No goes even earlier: No me levanto temprano.

With an infinitive, the pronoun can be attached to the end or placed before the whole phrase: Voy a ducharme = Me voy a duchar. It still matches the person: vamos a levantarnos.

Where the pronoun goes

Conjugated verb

no + me + ducho

Only before the verb. In the Perfecto — before haber: No me he duchado.

Infinitive

voy a ducharme

Attached to the end or placed before the first verb: Quiero sentarme = Me quiero sentar.

Gerund

estoy duchándome

The same two places: Estoy duchándome = Me estoy duchando. When you attach it, add a written accent: duchándome.

Commands

¡levántate! · ¡no te levantes!

In an affirmative command it is always attached, with an accent; in a negative one it goes before the verb. More at B1.

Position in use

  • Voy a ducharme ahora.I’m going to have a shower now.
  • No me levanto temprano los domingos.I don’t get up early on Sundays.
  • ¿Te vas a quedar en casa esta noche?Are you going to stay at home tonight?
  • No quiero enfadarme contigo.I don’t want to get angry with you.
  • ¡Un momento! Estamos vistiéndonos.Just a moment! We’re getting dressed.

How to remember

The pronoun never goes between the two verbs: voy a ducharme or me voy a duchar, but never “voy me a duchar”.

New meaning

The same verb can be reflexive or not, and the meaning changes: lavar el coche (to wash the car) — lavarse las manos (to wash your hands); llamar a Ana (to call Ana) — llamarse Ana (to be called Ana).

With body parts use the article, not “my”: Me lavo los dientes, not mis dientes. The same goes for clothes: Me pongo el abrigo.

Examples in pairs

  • Me lavo los dientes después de comer.I brush my teeth after eating.
  • Llamo a mi madre todos los días.I call my mother every day.
  • El bebé duerme diez horas.The baby sleeps for ten hours.
  • Anoche me dormí en el sofá.Last night I fell asleep on the sofa.
  • ¿Quedamos a las siete en la plaza?Shall we meet at seven in the square?
  • Hoy me quedo en casa.I’m staying at home today.
  • Hace frío: ponte el abrigo.It’s cold: put your coat on.
  • Mi hermana se parece mucho a mi padre.My sister looks a lot like my father.
