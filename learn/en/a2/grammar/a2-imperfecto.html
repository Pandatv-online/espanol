A2 · Grammar

Pretérito imperfecto

The past continuous — description, habit, the background of a story

Pretérito Imperfecto

When

Two past tenses

Imperfecto

era / tenía / vivía…

An unfinished, ongoing or repeated action in the past. It is the “background” of a story — what was going on when something happened.

Indefinido

fue / tuvo / vivió

An event with clear limits: it happened once, at a specific moment, and changed something. More in the “Contrast” tab.

Three main uses

Description

what it was like

era · estaba · tenía · hacía frío · había

Appearance, character, state, weather, the setting in the past. Time and age belong here too: Eran las tres. Tenía veinte años.

Habit

what people used to do

siempre · normalmente · a veces · todos los días · antes · cuando era niño

What people did regularly: in the past, as a child. In English often “used to” or “would”.

Background

what was already going on

mientras · cuando

What was already going on when an event happened. The event itself is in the indefinido.

Examples of the three uses

  • Era alto y tenía el pelo rubio.He was tall and had blond hair. (description)
  • Hacía frío y llovía mucho.It was cold and raining hard. (weather, setting)
  • De niño jugaba al fútbol todos los días.As a child I played football every day. (habit)
  • Antes vivíamos en Madrid.We used to live in Madrid. (before, but not any more)
  • Dormía cuando sonó el teléfono.I was sleeping when the phone rang. (background + event)
  • Llovía cuando salimos.It was raining when we went out. (background + event)

Important: state verbs (ser, estar, tener, querer, saber, poder, haber) are almost always in the imperfecto in descriptions of the past, not in the indefinido. The list and the exceptions are in the “Contrast” tab.

Polite requests: the imperfecto softens a wish, like English “I’d like”: Quería un café, por favor. · Quería preguntar una cosa.

Picture it

The imperfecto is a paused film: the background, the setting, the habits. The indefinido is a click: a specific event that changed something.

Conjugation

-ar endings: -aba, -abas, -aba, -ábamos, -abais, -aban. -er / -ir endings are the same: -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían. The stress on “a” and “í” is the key to the form. Switch between the groups on each card.

Good news: -er and -ir verbs share the same endings, and the whole language has only three irregular verbs in the imperfecto — ser, ir, ver. Even verbs that are irregular in the present are regular here: tener → tenía, poder → podía, hacer → hacía.

Written accents: -ar verbs have an accent only in the nosotros form (hablábamos); -er / -ir verbs have it in every form (comía, comíamos). The yo and él / ella forms are the same (hablaba), so add the pronoun when it is unclear who is acting.

Irregular

The three irregular verbs — ser, ir, ver — just need to be memorised. Ver keeps the “e” of its stem: veía, not “vía”.

Ser and ir are different in the imperfecto (era / iba) but identical in the indefinido (fui). Haber meaning “there is / there are” is regular too: hay → había — one form for singular and plural.

Ser, ir, ver in use

  • De niña era muy tímida.As a child I was very shy.
  • Todos los veranos íbamos al pueblo.Every summer we went to the village.
  • ¿Adónde ibas tan deprisa?Where were you going in such a hurry?
  • Antes veíamos muchas series juntos.We used to watch a lot of series together.
  • Desde la ventana se veía el mar.You could see the sea from the window.
  • En la plaza había mucha gente.There were a lot of people in the square.

Examples in pairs

  • Vivía en París.I lived in Paris. (description, background)
  • Se mudó a París.He moved to Paris. (a moment, a change)
  • Comía pizza los viernes.I used to eat pizza on Fridays. (regularly)

Background and event

Imperfecto

Together: the imperfecto sets the scene and describes the background (mientras, cuando); the indefinido is the event that bursts into that background or interrupts it.

Indefinido

De repente, entonces, en ese momento signal the indefinido: a turning point that interrupts the ongoing imperfecto background.

State verbs

Imperfecto

In descriptions of the past, state verbs are almost always in the imperfecto: ser → era · estar → estaba · tener → tenía · querer → quería · saber → sabía · poder → podía · haber → había · parecer → parecía · conocer → conocía.

Indefinido

Exception: the same verbs can be in the indefinido, but then the meaning changes: sabía (knew) → supe (found out) · quería (wanted) → quise (decided, tried) · podía (could) → pude (managed to) · conocía (knew someone) → conocí (met for the first time).

A change of meaning

  • Ya sabía la verdad.I already knew the truth.
  • Ayer supe la verdad.Yesterday I found out the truth.
  • Quería llamarte, pero no tenía tu número.I wanted to call you, but I didn’t have your number.
  • Quise abrir la puerta, pero estaba cerrada con llave.I tried to open the door, but it was locked.
  • No podíamos dormir por el ruido.We couldn’t sleep because of the noise.
  • Por fin pudimos dormir un poco.At last we managed to get a little sleep.

Markers

Markers

Imperfecto

siempre · normalmente · generalmente · a veces · casi siempre · todos los días · cada semana · antes · de niño / de niña · cuando era pequeño · mientras · en aquella época · frecuentemente · a menudo

Indefinido

ayer · anteayer · el lunes · de repente · entonces · en ese momento · de pronto · una vez · hace 3 años · el año pasado · en 2010 · aquella noche

Mientras is almost always followed by the imperfecto: two actions run at the same time, as background. Cuando works with both tenses — check whether it is background or an event.

A marker is a hint, not a law. Even ayer can go with the imperfecto when it describes the background: Ayer llovía cuando salí. — “It was raining yesterday when I went out.”

How to choose

Ask yourself: “What was it like? What was going on?” → imperfecto. “What happened?” → indefinido.

The action was repeated with no count (siempre, a menudo) → imperfecto. You can name the moment or the number of times (una vez, ayer a las ocho) → indefinido.

Examples

Regular verbs in use

  • Mi padre trabajaba en un banco.My father worked at a bank.
  • ¿Dónde vivíais antes?Where did you live before?
  • Los domingos comíamos en casa de los abuelos.On Sundays we had lunch at our grandparents’.

Description

  • La casa de mis abuelos tenía un jardín enorme.My grandparents’ house had a huge garden.
  • El hotel estaba cerca de la playa y era muy barato.The hotel was near the beach and was very cheap.
  • Eran las diez de la noche y todavía hacía calor.It was ten at night and still hot.

Habit

  • En la universidad estudiabais por la noche, ¿verdad?At university you studied at night, didn’t you?
  • Mi madre nos leía un cuento cada noche.My mother read us a story every night.
  • Antes fumaba, pero ya no.I used to smoke, but not any more.

Background and politeness

  • Mientras yo cocinaba, los niños veían la tele.While I was cooking, the kids were watching TV.
  • Íbamos por la autopista cuando se pinchó una rueda.We were driving on the motorway when we got a flat tyre.
  • ¿Qué hacías cuando te llamé?What were you doing when I called you?
  • Quería un café con leche, por favor.I’d like a white coffee, please.

A mini story with both tenses

Era una noche tranquila. Llovía y hacía frío. Yo estaba en casa y leía un libro cuando, de repente, sonó el teléfono. Me levanté, contesté y escuché la voz de mi amigo.

Italics mark the imperfecto (background), bold marks the indefinido (events).

The story line by line

  • Era una noche tranquila. Llovía y hacía frío.It was a quiet night. It was raining and it was cold.
  • Yo estaba en casa y leía un libro cuando, de repente, sonó el teléfono.I was at home reading a book when suddenly the phone rang.
  • Me levanté, contesté y escuché la voz de mi amigo.I got up, answered and heard my friend’s voice.
